# How to Get a Job

By the Headless Headhunter. Notes transcribed from the "Resume guide 2.0" PDF, plus the one-page example resume it ships with. Reference only; not published (see `exclude` in `_config.yml`).

## How the corporate world hires

A company with an open position gives a manager budget for a new hire: the Hiring Manager (HM). Hiring is rarely their whole job and most HMs have little hiring experience, so they engage Recruiters to manage the open positions.

Hiring process (a "candidate" is a person looking for a job):

1. HM gets budget for a new role
2. HM engages a Recruiter
3. Recruiter gathers the qualifications (keywords) for the role from the HM
4. Recruiter posts a job posting to a job board (Indeed, LinkedIn, Monster, etc.)
5. Recruiter reviews the resumes candidates send for the qualifications the HM wants
6. Resumes pass a pre-screen (optional) and get sent to the HM
7. The HM interviews candidates
8. An offer goes to the best candidate. If no candidate meets the HM's criteria, the process restarts at step 5 (an HM would rather not hire than make a "bad hire").

## What a resume is

- The resume's only job is to get interviews. A good resume gets more interviews than a bad one.
- Good resumes read like government forms you turn in at the DMV.
- Resumes are made to show you meet the **minimum qualifications** for the role, and are tailored to a specific job title.
- They are terrible at brags, marketing, and graphic design.

How to grade a resume: interviews per applications sent.

| Ratio    | Grade |
| -------- | ----- |
| 1 to 10  | A+    |
| 1 to 25  | B     |
| 1 to 50  | C-    |
| 1 to 75  | D     |
| 1 to 100+| F     |

## The most important step: find the keywords

Before writing anything, find the keywords for your job title. Keywords are shorthand for the qualifications that both the Recruiter and HM look for. Each has about 15 seconds with your resume, and has to see **how** you used a keyword and **why** in that time, or they reject you.

**Job title**: the common name for a set of qualifications. For example:

- Barista: POS, Customer Service, Basic Math, Multitasking
- Accountant: Excel, GAAP, CPA
- IT Helpdesk: Customer Service, Troubleshooting, MS Office, Zoom, Windows
- Project Manager: PMP/CSM, Client/Stakeholders, Agile, Cross Functional

**Job description (JD)**: holds the title, salary, day-to-day duties, and EEOC/EOE statements. Ignore all of it except the qualifications.

**Qualifications** are the bullet points under sections like:

- Requirements
- Qualifications
- Must Have
- Need to Have
- What we are Looking for
- We Would Love To Meet You If
- You Might Be a Great Fit If

A JD with keywords in those sections is a Good JD; one without is a Bad JD.

### How to find the right qualifications

1. Go to Indeed, LinkedIn, Monster, etc. to find jobs.
2. Find a Good JD where you meet 75% of the qualifications. Copy the keywords to a sheet or spreadsheet.
3. Repeat 10 to 15 more times for JDs with the same job title.
4. Mark how many times each qualification appeared. The more it appeared, the more important it is.
5. That list is what you build the resume from.

Repeat for every job title you apply to. Resumes are job-title specific: an amazing Project Manager resume fails for a Barista job and vice versa.

A resume can be made in Microsoft Word or Google Docs; neither is better.

## Formatting

Basic rules:

- Font: Arial, no other font
- Black and white only (phone, email, and LinkedIn/portfolio may be blue)
- Single column only
- Line spacing 1.5
- Longer than one page is fine
- No picture of yourself

### Section 1: name, contact, location

Center aligned.

- First and last name: size 14, bold
- Phone, email, LinkedIn/portfolio: size 12
- "US Citizen" / "Green Card Holder" (if you are one) goes with city and state: size 12
- Languages spoken can go beside or below the last line

### Optional: summary

Don't label it "Summary"; put it directly below the contact information. Only write one if you are:

- **Changing industries**: e.g. "I am transitioning from Wind Turbine Mechanic to Fry Cook from Y"
- **Relocating** for the job: e.g. "Moving to CITY on X-Date but can move sooner if needed"
- **Needing sponsorship or on a visa**: explain how people can work with your visa, since Recruiters and HMs may not know they can hire you. On an H1B without an approved I-140, or on OPT, you don't need this summary.

Otherwise, no summary.

### Section 2: education and certificates (optional)

- Left aligned, size 10.5
- "Education & Certificates" is bold; nothing else in the section is
- No longer than 3 lines, all in bullet points
- Degree (B.A., M.A., PhD, etc.), then the subject, then where
- Graduated more than 3 years ago: write "Status - Graduated". Otherwise write the graduation year.
- Relevant certificates: list them; no date needed while still active
- If the job doesn't require a degree, or you'd be massively over-qualified, move this section to the bottom of the resume

### Section 3: work history, internships, projects

- Work history and internships go under **Work History**; projects go under **Projects**
- Paid by a company: work history. Not paid by a company: project.
- Projects have no dates and get 3 bullet points maximum
- Order of importance: relevance to the job, then work history, then internships, then projects
- Size 10.5; only the section name ("Work History", "Projects") is bold
- Reverse chronological order, left aligned

**Job information line**: italicize job title, company, and state (left) and the dates (right). Full-time work and internships must show months and years. If still employed: "Month Year to Current".

**Job summary**: one bullet that says what you did at this job.

- Includes a few keywords from your list
- Basic enough for someone with no industry knowledge to understand
- 3 lines maximum, no more than 1 period
- Past tense, even if still employed

**Past experience bullets**: What, How, Result/Reason.

- Include as many keywords from your list as you can
- Show HOW you used the keyword and the result or reason you used it (they often overlap; that's fine)
- 3 lines maximum, no more than 1 period, past tense
- Example 1: What: customer service, multitasking. How: handled a large lunch line by performing customer service and multitasking. Result: 5-star Google reviews.
- Example 2: What: basic math. How: added and subtracted cash. Reason: gave customers correct change.

Repeat with different keywords until you have 3 to 8 bullets per job, counting the summary (1 summary + 2 to 7 What/How/Result bullets), depending on how long you were there.

Then repeat for the next job. Include no more than 12 years of experience this way (except high-level directors and managers). Goal: 75% of your keyword list appears in the first half of the first page. Then it's ready to send.

## Where to apply

Jobs are posted to job boards by Recruiters and HMs.

- LinkedIn.com and Indeed.com are the primary sites; they hold most jobs on the market
- Others: Monster.com, ZipRecruiter.com, Glassdoor.com, craigslist.org
- Other boards mostly repost what's on Indeed and LinkedIn
- Craigslist jobs are 95% scams, but the 5% real ones appear nowhere else

### The #1 rule: apply faster

The faster you apply, the better your chances of an interview.

- Recruiters have 35 to 70 open positions at once and use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) to track requisitions and where each candidate is (first interview, second interview, offer, rejected)
- An ATS sorts people in the order they applied. Anything that delays you hitting "submit" lowers your chances. If you're resume #139 and the recruiter finds who they need at #75, then once the interview schedules fill up they stop looking unless the HM needs more candidates.
- AI-based ATS and resume scanning exist but are rare: unless you apply to an AI company, about 1 in 100 applications
- The default for most ATSs (Workday, ADP, Taleo) is first-come, first-served

### Searching

- On Indeed and LinkedIn, search by job title and a location you can commute to
- For remote roles, set the location to "United States", not where you live, or you'll see fewer jobs
- The guide shows how to apply faster than others on Indeed and LinkedIn only as screenshots (which buttons to click); those steps are not transcribed here

### LinkedIn AI search

- As of 8/20/2026 LinkedIn has an AI search that is less powerful than the classic version; switch back to classic if you can
- If you can't, search for `JOB NAME jobs with under 100 applicants` in the search box to find jobs with under 100 applicants

## FAQ

- **Why is it so hard to find a job now?** There's a recession: more people need jobs than jobs exist. Every problem with job searching comes down to the economy.
- **What application-to-interview ratio can you get me?** About 1 interview per 7 applications for non-remote roles, 1 per 20 for remote-only, and 1 per 100 for internationals (most internationals see 1 in 400).
- **Private lessons?** None; join the livestream M/W/F at 11:00 PST on YouTube or Twitch for a resume review.
- **Does this work for XXX job?** Yes, from accountants to zebra handlers. The exceptions are lawyers and medical doctors.
- **Interviews guide?** Not yet. Interviews depend on who you're talking to and what they want.
- **Ghost jobs?** They don't exist; the articles all trace back to resume or job-search sites that want your money. Scams do exist. If you ever have to pay to get a job, run.
- **Does it work in XXX country?** Yes: USA, Canada, Mexico, India, the EU, the UK, Africa and more. The only exception is the Netherlands.
- **Can I thank you?** Yes, with a tip, and a testimonial with the resume that got you the job.

## Example resume (the target format)

Single column, Arial, centered header, section names bold, job lines italic with dates right-aligned.

```
                          Lee Korelitz
        123-456-1234 | myemail@email.com | www.linkedin.com/in/leekorelitz
                    US Citizen in Los Angeles, CA

Education & Certificates
 ● B.A. in Economics from NYC College in NY                Status - Graduated

Work History
Cashier at Large Ducks Coffee, TX                          June 2023 to Current
 ● Operated our Point of Sale (POS) cash register to collect money from
   customers and performed basic math to give them the proper change, keeping
   them engaged with customer service while I prepared their food and beverages.
 ● Handled a large lunch rush line by providing customer service and
   multitasking while making their food, which resulted in multiple five-star
   reviews on our Google Page mentioning me by name.
 ● Gave customers correct change by adding and subtracting cash.
 ● Used a desktop computer to read company emails, take online barista training
   courses, and input shift availability into our schedule.
 ● Arrived on time to every shift and found coverage for my shift when an
   emergency stopped me from coming into work.

Projects
Waiter at Shake and Bake Doughnuts                         October 2021 to June 2023
 ● Spoke with customers to get their orders and delivered them to our kitchen
   staff via our POS, ensured customers had a good time with customer service,
   and kept everything cleaned and up to code.
 ● Took customers' orders and performed customer service (Active Listening) for
   multiple tables of customers.
 ● Upsold drink sizes and gave customers correct change when asked for the bill
   by performing addition and subtraction if they paid in cash rather than a
   credit card.
 ● Took directions from management on which areas needed extra help when we were
   short staffed.
```

Note: the example files a waiter job (a paid role, with dates) under "Projects", which contradicts the guide's own rule that paid work goes under Work History. Follow the rule, not the example.
